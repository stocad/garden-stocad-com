title: Career Goals and New beginnings
---
My first annual review in corporate America was a bit of a mess. I didn't understand what the process meant and the questions implied a lot of soul searching and essay writing where my manager encouraged me to time box my review to a couple hours. I think I spent most of a day working on it, but looking back, it doesn't show.

Retrospecting on your past years activity is healthy, however doing so at the request of an employer can feel really dangerous. Should I be critical of myself? If I'm not critical in the way my manager expects, will that count against me? Will my own words be used to hurt my career later on? It is not my intent to fully explore this here, maybe seedling for future reflections.

Back on that first review in March of 2009 there was a vague question posed as "What are your career goals?". I remember looking at that simple question as a deep chasm. What do I want to do with my life? At the time, I wrote:

> I would like to run my own IT shop with a focus on building applications and consulting for small businesses and non-profits.

My current job is the closest I've come to this, building open source developer tools that help accelerate front end development projects, which includes a decent amount of consulting with users of the library I work on.

I've been thinking about a career change and in asking myself what I want to do next I recalled this answer I gave 16 years ago as an unfulfilled ambition that still reflects what I want to be working on.

My answer is memorable all these years later not only because it remains true, but also because it was wrong. It was clear in my managers response that this wasn't at all what he was looking for. I received the feedback that I should have come up with one or more SMART goals that I could turn into an action plan to assess the skills and experiences needed to achieve them.

He's probably right and maybe it's time to apply SMART to this old career goal and start working on it. 

-----

SMART goals[^1] are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound.

----

### Smarter goal setting

In the next 3 months, I have time set aside to dedicate to learning. Everything AI related is really valuable right now and whether the market is over its ski's or not at the moment, the AI maximalism trend will ensure AI is set to ensure all of this will be relevant decades from now. Similarly, I am well positioned both in background as well as support to work through the AWS Certification program.

Related SMART goals I'll consider are:

>By May 15 2025, complete an AI development course and document the learning through a technical knowledge garden system (here), creating at least 15 interconnected notes covering core concepts, practical applications, and personal insights.

>By the end of June 2025 take the test to become a AWS Certified Developer – Associate, including taking the Foundational Cloud Practitioner prereq. While studying for these tests create at minimum 20 notes/articles documenting knowledge gained and the process involved.

Learning is great, but I need to establish a social media presence as a foundation from which to measure ideas and an audience to eventually be my 1000 true fans.

>Establish a weekly cross-posting strategy for technical garden content across platforms such as Twitter, Instagram, LinkedIn, and DEV.to, with specialized formatting for each platform to maximize engagement. Schedule specific time each week to draft and schedule content. Measure conversion and website engagement. At the end of June, wild success would be to have 1,000 weekly visitors to any stocad.com domain website.

>By the end of July create a pilot series of 3-5 "Show Me Something Cool" micro-interviews (5-15 minutes) with developers showcasing interesting tools or techniques. Before posting the first episode, line up an additional 5 interviews that will keep content stable after the initial 5 are posted.

Last, at the end of July we need to have a product worth building

> By the end of May 2025, summarize and stack rank 50 project ideas here in this public digital garden.

>By the end of June 2025, produce a product analysis by performing detailed market research and niche product market fit for each of the top 10 product ideas.

>By the end of July 2025, write a detailed product requirements document (PRD) for the top 3 ideas from the product analysis step.

Is that all? Nope, this is just the beginning. Once enough of these goals are on track I'll need to have goals around measuring market interest, forming a business, building a product and communicating my progress along the way.

### Conclusion
Back in 2009 I had an idea about where I wanted to invest my energy. I haven't done much to honor this in the meantime. Since that time, the world has changed a lot and the software industry is resting on the edge of a revolution. 

When cable TV flooded the entertainment market with channels due to having an amazing amount more bandwidth than broadcast TV, we all gained the ability to watch show much closer to our interest. Streaming changed the market again where now any person can watch any show, beginning at the precise moment they want.

Software is already starting through this same process where businesses that have been satisfied using Outlook or Salesforce are going to start considering using products more tailored for their business use-case. Why be satisfied with good enough for your daily software tasks when its inexpensive to have your tool purpose built to your needs? Cost is why this hasn't happened yet and the floor is dropping out from under all of the incumbent solution providers.
